TAPEDECK

Play back any DBC launch. Fly it again under a different config. Or take the stick and trade it yourself. On an engine that is checked against the chain.

SIDE A

A1

DBC makes every launch parameter a choice. There is no way to hear a choice before it meets real money.

Curve shape, launch fee, who gets the fee, who owns the liquidity after migration: a launchpad sets all of it in one config, once, and then thousands of launches live with it. The tools for choosing are calculators. They show the curve. They do not show what real order flow does on it: the bundle in the launch slot, the wallets that sell into the first minute, the buyers who arrive late.

A2

A tape is a real launch, order by order. The deck plays it.

  • Side A plays the launch on the curve it happened on.
  • Side B flies the same orders through any config: one built with the SDK's own curve builders, or any config on mainnet, loaded by address. Every buyer spends the same SOL at the same moment; every seller sells the same share of what they hold by then. The ledger shows who paid and who collected, cohort by cohort, under each.
  • Any pool: paste a DBC pool address. Its transactions are fetched in your browser, every swap is replayed against the events the program emitted, and the pool goes on the deck.
  • REC gives you a paper wallet. Your orders go onto the tape at the moment you press, and the rest of the launch trades against the price you left behind.
  • A config you like leaves as a file for Meteora's Invent CLI, or goes to mainnet from the deck: it asks the live program whether it takes the design (a simulation, no wallet), then creates the config from your own wallet.

The shelf holds 84 tapes: 24 DBC pools read from the chain and 60 pump.fun launches recorded wallet by wallet.

A3

It is not a simulator's guess. It is checked.

116,724real mainnet swaps replayed from pool creation, across 355 pools and 50 kinds of config
0mismatches, comparing amount in, amount out, next price and every fee, to the lamport
0.24%worst gap between replayed and recorded tokens on nine buys in ten, on any pump.fun tape on the shelf

Do not take the number on trust: paste a pool of your own into the deck and it runs the same check in front of you.

History only covers the swaps people make, and nobody makes exact-out swaps. So those were put to the program itself: 2,345 transactions simulated against the live program on 262 open pools, 2,601 exact-out swaps, 0 disagreements. Nothing signed, nothing sent.

Prices and fees come from Meteora's SDK. Around them the engine does what the program does and the SDK leaves out: the volatility tracker before and after each swap, the fee buckets, the minimum-fee first swap, completion at the threshold. A pump.fun tape is kept only if a DBC config equal to pump.fun's curve reproduces its recorded fills: of 1,206 recorded, 584 were too short to play, 61 failed the check and 561 passed.

SIDE B

B1

What the deck found: a graduation only means something where it was expensive to fake.

The cheapest way onto every "just migrated" feed is to buy your own curve in the launch transaction. The engine prices that round trip for any config: buy the curve, take back what the config hands a creator, sell out. Then every config and pool on mainnet was read, 1,768,464 pools under 543,738 configs.

84%of 1,131,326 pools completed, where faking costs under 2% of the raise
2.7%of 438,317 pools completed, where it costs 10% or more
78 of 121pump.fun graduations recorded 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-11 were one party buying the curve in the launch slot

On pump.fun's own curve the round trip costs about 19 SOL, mostly the fifth of the raise that stays behind in the pool. A config decides that number. Tapedeck shows it next to every design, and shows what the same buyout tape would have paid under yours.

B2

Who it is for

  • Launchpad builders: hear a config before shipping it, against the flow it will actually meet, and export it.
  • Terminals: one number per config that says what a graduation under it cost. The census ships as a JSON file.
  • Traders: practice on real launches with no money and an honest ledger. Most cohorts lose; the deck shows which.
  • Meteora: a conformance corpus for the SDK. The verified histories ship as one 20 MB file, and any build of the swap math replays through it in about a second. The 2.0 branch (PRs #123 to #126) was run through it on 11 October: 116,724 swaps, 0 mismatches.
B3

Where it stops, and what is next

  • A replay is a stress test, not a forecast: it prices the play on the tape under your config, it does not predict how traders would adapt. REC is the honest way to try.
  • After migration the pool is modelled as constant product with the config's flat fee.
  • The tapes cover days, not seasons.
  • Measured on the way: in SDK 1.5.13 getPoolConfigsByOwner filters on the leftover receiver, not the fee claimer. The SDK team opened a fix the same day (PR #124). The census shows how far it reaches: the two differ for 37% of configs on mainnet (200,278).
  • Also found: the Invent config's comments size slot-based fee schedules at 0.4 s a slot. Mainnet slots ran at 0.219 s on 2026-10-10, so a schedule written as "60 seconds" in slots lasts about 33. The deck writes schedules in seconds.
  • Next: a live recorder for new DBC pools, tapes for stock-quoted launches, and the migrated pool's own fee scheduler.
